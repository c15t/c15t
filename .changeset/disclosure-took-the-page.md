`ConsentIabDrawer` renders the IAB TCF disclosure as a full page instead of a card. The web puts it in a centred dialog, which on a phone is the worst of both: the app behind is dimmed and unreadable, and a list that runs to 168 partners still does not fit the room it was given. So this is a fourth `ConsentSurface` presentation, `drawer`, and not a second sheet implementation -- the header, the scrolling body, the sticky footer, the safe-area bands, Android's back button, reduced motion, and the modal accessibility treatment are the ones the banner and the dialog already use. The card slides in from the trailing edge and takes the whole width, and `presentation` is fixed rather than offered, because a host that could pick `dialog` here could pick the thing the surface exists to avoid.

It renders from props and reads no consent kernel. `model` is a name-for-name mirror of the display model `@c15t/iab` publishes: `ConsentIabDisplayModel` mirrors `HeadlessIABDialogDisplayModel`, `ConsentIabProcessedVendor` mirrors `HeadlessIABProcessedVendor`, and each row carries the web's own `testId` rather than one derived here. `purpose-item-1` names a purpose, a special purpose, a feature and a special feature at once, so an id scheme worked out twice is an id scheme wrong twice. `iab-display-parity.test.ts` builds the same rows with the web's own resolver and `iabDisplayTestId` and compares field for field, in row order, against the fixture the drawer tests and the example app render. The drawer ships no runtime dependency on `@c15t/core`, `c15t`, or `@iabtechlabtcf/core`; `@c15t/iab` is a devDependency of that test and nothing else.

Both tabs match the web's. Purposes leads with the rows a subject decides on -- purpose 1, the standalone purposes, stacks, special features -- and closes with the locked essential section on and non-interactive. A stack names the purposes it absorbed, and a partner row keeps its consent leg and its legitimate-interest leg apart, opening to the purposes it claims under each. Every switch is local until Save: one press calls `onSave` once with the whole selection, including the rows nobody moved, so the far side of the bridge gets a set it can encode rather than four partial maps to default. Closing writes nothing, and `Reject All` and `Accept All` are decisions that write rather than drafts that do not.

The measurement follows the web, at 411 by 914 and light: a 32pt tab in a 40pt segmented control, 12 padding inside a row header, 8 between purpose rows, a 35.5pt action, 16 between the two action rows and 16 inside the pair, 12 and 16 of footer padding. Two numbers are recorded rather than smoothed over. The drawer's card takes no inset, where the web's sits 16 off the viewport, because that 16 is `.root { padding: 16 }` around a centred dialog. And one gap part serves both row lists at 8, where `.vendorListItem` separates by 6, so partner rows land 2 apart on pitch; the metrics scripts print both.

`initialTab` is what lets a host copy the web's `{count} partners` link, which opens the same disclosure straight on the partner list. TCF data is still not on the native snapshot -- the protocol reserves an `iab` slot and holds it `null` -- so the rows are yours to supply until it is.

`examples/react-native-bare` mounts the drawer behind a partner-count link on its privacy screen, driven by `c15t-demo://iab`, and both measurement scripts take an `iab-drawer` mode that grades what passed carries back against the web figures above: `--mode iab-drawer` on Android reads the rows out of a view tree, including the pitch, and on iOS reads the card inset, tabs, actions and footer off pixels and prints the rows as NOT MEASURED rather than guessing at a divider.
