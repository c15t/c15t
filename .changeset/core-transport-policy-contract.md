Carry the v3 policy contract and category receipts through the transports. Every backend-bound request sends `x-c15t-policy-contract: 1` next to `x-c15t-version`. `mapInitOutputToInitResponse` returns the raw `policyResolution` wire for the kernel's strict reader; a producer that declares no contract has its legacy `policy` field lifted through the named bridge, a negotiated producer whose response lacks the field is `failed`/`invalid-payload`, and a producer declaring a contract this client does not speak is `failed`/`unsupported-contract` whatever its body says. `Sec-GPC` is reported as `resolvedPrivacySignals.gpc` (exact values only) and no longer folded into `overrides.gpc`. Server prefetch clears a stale policy, decision, snapshot token and IAB seed whenever the resolution is not `matched`.

`buildSubjectPostBody` sends `choice` with only the receipts the action confirmed, keeping the kernel's confirmation times and bases, and fills `preferences` from the explicit receipt values rather than effective permissions. The hosted and manifest transports gain `loadSubjectRecord(subjectId)`, which maps the backend's subject read (or a 2.x backend's granted codes) onto hydration records, and `recordPrivacyOptOut(directive, subjectId)`, which uses the privacy-directive route and never the consent route. A 2.x-shaped save response is now a successful save instead of a queued failure, and a failed manifest fetch is no longer cached across init retries. `identify` before a server subject exists resolves at once and sends nothing, in the hosted and manifest transports alike: the transport keeps no pending promise and remembers no subject of its own, the next save carries the identity, and the kernel forwards standing directives to the subject it establishes. The subject id the kernel passes is the only subject a transport acts on. A custom endpoint transport without an `init` handler reports an explicitly `unconfigured` resolution.

`createOfflineTransport` accepts `policyRules` (resolved once per init with `resolvePolicyRules`) or legacy `policyPacks`, and always emits an explicit `policyResolution`.
