Consolidate the consent kernel onto one choice and effective-permission model. The snapshot now exposes `explicitChoice` (one receipt per optional category with its confirmation time and policy basis), `effectivePermissions` (what every gate consumes), `promptRequirement`, `noticeDismissal`, `privacySignals`, `optOutDirectives`, `resolution`, `policyRule`, `restrictions`, `nextDeadline`, `subject`, and `evaluatedAt`. `consents` is now an alias of `effectivePermissions`, `hasConsented` only reports that a receipt exists, and neither gates UI or saves.

Only `commands.save()` records a choice. `'all'` and `'none'` confirm exactly the active policy scope, an object confirms exactly its own optional keys, and a no-input save confirms the presented selection (staged draft, then explicit value, then the model's displayed default) rather than masked permissions. Invalid input is rejected atomically, an empty patch records nothing, a repeated unchanged value refreshes only the supplied receipts, and the action time is captured once and reused unchanged on a queued replay. `SavePayload` carries the complete `choice`, `confirmed: { categories, actionAt }`, and `subject`. `commands.dismissNotice()` records a local notice dismissal without touching permissions. `kernel.hydrate(records)` applies validated records without a choice event, `kernel.refresh(now)` re-evaluates on demand, and `set.privacySignals({ gpc })` carries the detected browser signal separately from the `overrides.gpc` developer override.

Positive grants expire after the policy's choice validity; denials and recorded opt-out directives stay effective. A detected GPC signal masks the categories the policy maps to it and records a standing `source: 'gpc'` directive once the lifecycle has started; removing the signal does not remove the directive. Policy resolution is read from the versioned `policyResolution` wire (legacy `policy` is lifted through the schema bridge), and `unconfigured`, `no-match`, and `failed` outcomes all clear policy-derived state and use the safe opt-in fallback while the status stays observable. A failed init never keeps a permissive policy. Nothing hashes, writes, or schedules timers during construction; the expiry deadline timer is installed by `init` and `hydrate`, clamps long delays, and stops on `dispose()`.

Kernel events are split into `choice:recorded`, `permissions:changed`, `notice:dismissed`, and `privacy:opt-out`; `consent:set` is removed.

Persistence writes the v3 record only on `choice:recorded`, and writes `<key>-notice` and `<key>-privacy` records (localStorage plus a compact cookie projection) on their own events. Hydration reads the first structurally valid record (cookie, configured key, legacy key), keeps legacy receipts, subject identifiers, and times exactly, and never writes. `readStoredRecordsFromCookieHeader(cookieHeader, storageConfig, now)` reads choice, notice, and privacy records with one clock for `KernelConfig.initialRecords`, so a server render and the client produce the same initial snapshot. `clear()` cancels queued writes, removes every c15t record, and resets the kernel. Pending initialization cannot restore cleared records or an earlier subject. Privacy forwarding retries failed requests with the original directive timestamp and tracks successful acknowledgements separately for each subject.

Bridges kept for adapters until the final sweep: `set.consent()` stages a draft only, `set.hasConsented()` is inert, `initialConsents` seeds the draft, `initialHasConsented` is ignored, a legacy `initialPolicy` without `initialPolicyResolution` stays hidden until a no-transport init lifts it, `readStoredConsentFromCookie` still returns the legacy payload, and `getConsentAvailableCategories` plus the `Consent` type remain on `@c15t/core/consent-record`.
