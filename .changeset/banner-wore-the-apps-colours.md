Draw the built-in surfaces with the same tokens as the web. The accent is the web blue, `#335CFF` and `#6685FF` in dark, where the package had been filling buttons near-black, and the neutrals, corners, spacing, and type scale now match `packages/ui`: text `#1A1A1A`, muted `#666666`, border `#E6E6E6`, 12pt cards and 8pt controls, and a 4/8/16/24/32 step. Actions are outlined rather than filled, the banner carries the web hairline and `0 8px 24px rgba(0, 0, 0, 0.12)` elevation, and its actions sit on the muted `#FAFAFA` footer band with a rule above them. `Reject All` and `Accept All` share a row at equal width in the accent, with `Customize` full width below in the neutral outline, so the tertiary action is no longer a text link wedged beside two buttons. A category switch draws the 28x16 small track the consent surfaces ask the web primitive for, with its 10pt thumb, instead of tinting the platform `Switch`, and keeps the 44pt tap target. Text parts now apply the weight their theme declares, which never reached a device while it travelled under the name `weight`.

The banner and the sheet no longer share one heading-and-body pair, because the web does not share one either: a banner heading leads its copy and a sheet heading follows it. `ConsentThemeTypography` gains `bannerTitle` (16/24 at 500) and `bannerBody` (14/20 at 400), and keeps `title` and `body` at the dialog values (a 14 semibold heading on its own 14pt line over 16/24), and a button label is 14 medium on the web tight line at 17.5. A host that hands in a whole `ConsentTheme` literal has to spell the two new roles; `createConsentTheme` still accepts a partial. `ConsentThemeColors` loses `secondary` and `onSecondary`, which had stopped being read the moment the secondary action became an outline.

A category is now its own bordered card, separated from the next by 12pt rather than divided by a rule, and the switch stays pinned to its right edge. Descriptions remain visible inside the card: the web accordion hides them behind a disclosure, and an extra tap to read what you are consenting to is wrong on a phone. That difference is deliberate and is the remaining visual gap alongside the branding tag, which web hangs above the banner card and this package does not draw.

Both sheets now offer the two decisions the web sheets offer. `ConsentDialog` and `ConsentPreferences` put `Reject All` and `Accept All` in an accent row above `Save Settings`, so granting or denying everything no longer means moving every switch. Either one writes the whole grant or the minimum and closes the surface; the switches left mid-edit are dropped rather than saved, and a write the core refuses keeps the sheet open with the draft intact.

Category and button labels follow the web list: `We value your privacy`, `Accept All`, `Reject All`, `Save Settings`, `Privacy Settings`, `Analytics`, and `Strictly Necessary`.
