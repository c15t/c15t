`consentManifest()` from `c15t/vue/vite` downloads the policy when
`vite build` or `vite dev` starts:

<include src="../examples/vue/vite-config.mdx" />

Without a `backendURL` option, the plugin reads `VITE_C15T_BACKEND_URL` from
the environment or `.env`. `manifest()` from `c15t/vue/vue-plugin`, with no
options, reads the downloaded policy and the same backend URL, so `src/main.ts`
needs no import from `c15t/generated`:

<include src="../examples/vue/main.mdx" />

The browser resolves the visitor's policy from the bundled snapshot, with no
`/manifest` or `/init` request. Without `inputs` or `geoURL`, every visitor
gets the policy your project assigns when the location is unknown, so
configure that fallback rule. The app bundles the policy and English copy,
and loads the resolver as its own chunk when it starts.

When the build has no snapshot, as in `vite dev` after a failed download,
`manifest()` fetches `${backendURL}/manifest` when the app starts.
