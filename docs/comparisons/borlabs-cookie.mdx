## Keep control when your product extends beyond WordPress

c15t is the stronger fit when your team needs consent in a custom web application. Its framework components and backend let you own the consent system independently of WordPress.

Borlabs Cookie is a WordPress product with local visitor data, individual service controls and developer tools. Self-hosting alone does not distinguish c15t from Borlabs.

## Compare what you get

| What you need | c15t | Borlabs Cookie |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Build outside WordPress | Native web framework adapters, browser and headless options | WordPress only |
| Control the interface | Themes, component parts and headless APIs | Dialog settings, custom content placeholders and JavaScript API |
| Let visitors choose services | Category and individual vendor state | Service groups with individual service choices |
| Control scripts and embeds | Declared rules for scripts, requests and embeds | Script, style and content controls |
| Keep records on your servers | Deploy the supplied c15t backend | Consent logs within the WordPress setup. Visitor data stays on your server |
| Use a managed backend | Inth hosts the backend and stores records in its database. Self-hosting is optional | Consent runs within your WordPress installation |
| Discover services | **No automatic scanner in the packages** | Scanner with suggested service packages |
| Operate within WordPress | Developer setup. No bundled WordPress wizard | Setup assistant, package library, WP CLI and Composer support |

Sources: [Borlabs product and platform scope](https://borlabs.io/), [feature list](https://borlabs.io/borlabs-cookie/features/) and [JavaScript API](https://borlabs.io/docs/javascript-api-v3/).

## Connect consent to your application

Borlabs already covers several reasons teams choose a consent tool: local data, service choices, embeds and consent logs. Its API also supports custom integrations.

c15t adds a [native workflow across web frameworks](/docs/frameworks). Your team can put privacy controls into a product screen and connect them to shared consent state.

Use [headless APIs](/docs/frameworks/react/headless) for your markup. Declare [vendors](/docs/frameworks/react/vendor-consent) beside the integrations they control.

The backend is a separate service that can run with your chosen database. That separation helps when the application frontend and consent service need their own deployment lifecycle.

## Choose where administration belongs

Choose c15t when your main product is a custom web application or extends beyond a WordPress site. Keep consent in the product's code review and release process.

Borlabs can be the better fit when your team wants consent administration inside WordPress. Its scanner and package library can reduce the manual configuration of supported plugins and services.

With either product, your team must check new integrations and consent changes. Local data storage does not remove the work of updates, backups and operational checks.

## What c15t does not include

- The packages do not include a WordPress administration screen, setup assistant or automatic service scanner.
- They do not generate privacy policies or manage wider legal-document work.
- Vendor rules cover configured targets. They do not discover every tag or CMS plugin automatically.
- A browser choice does not prove that the backend saved the record.

Some integrations load with denied signals. Withdrawal normally reloads the page, and optional cleanup cannot remove all vendor data.

Include setup and backend operation when you compare the total work and cost.

## Test the application boundary

Follow the [framework guide](/docs/frameworks). Connect one vendor and one embed that your product needs outside WordPress.

Test refusal, acceptance, reload and withdrawal with the [verification guide](/docs/guides/verify-consent). Check requests and records. Check any tags that the CMS still supplies too.
