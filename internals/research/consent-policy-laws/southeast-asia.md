# SEA cookie policy review, 8 September 2026

Research decisions for ordinary websites, not whole-country compliance certification. Named opt-in presets describe consent-based optional tracking. They must not claim every cookie in a country legally needs a banner. An engine opt-out default allows processing immediately; it neither obtains implied consent nor runs a legitimate-interest assessment.

Across these eight: I found no primary-source mandate to recognize the US GPC protocol. This is an absence finding, not proof no future rule or specific request can apply. Keep visible withdrawal/refusal controls; do not equate GPC absence with consent. A no-banner setup cannot enable all optional categories just because a country permits some nonconsent processing.

## Singapore

Primary cookie guidance, revised May 2024, pp 54-55, paragraphs 7.5 and 7.7-7.14:
https://www.pdpc.gov.sg/-/media/files/pdpc/pdf-files/advisory-guidelines/ag-on-selected-topics/advisory-guidelines-on-the-pdpa-for-selected-topics-(revised-may-2024).pdf
It regulates cookies collecting personal data, not device storage as such. Nonpersonal cookies need no consent. Requested and informed activities such as authentication, security, preferences, network management and streaming can proceed on voluntary provision. Browser settings can express consent when actively chosen; default browser acceptance/inactivity cannot. Paragraph 7.13 puts the duty on the collecting organization, including collection through intermediaries; where an independent third party alone collects, that collector must obtain consent. Paragraph 7.14 requires consent for personal-data ad targeting and recommends in-site preferences. This does not establish that every analytics event is consent-only.

PDPC Key Concepts, actual downloaded document revised 29 April 2026 despite old URL filename:
https://www.pdpc.gov.sg/-/media/files/pdpc/pdf-files/advisory-guidelines/ag-on-key-concepts/advisory-guidelines-on-key-concepts-in-the-pdpa-17-may-2022.pdf
Pages 46-51 explain PDPA section 15A: assess and mitigate adverse effects, provide effective notice of purposes and a reasonable opt-out period, and wait until it expires. Examples use ten or fourteen days, not statutory universal periods. An exhibition's immediate sensor capture after venue notice fails because no reasonable period exists. Direct marketing messages are excluded; express opt-in generally required. Legitimate-interest/business-improvement exceptions exist, with their own limits. Page-load permission cannot represent this notification route. Withdrawal remains available after expiry. Overseas transfers require comparable protection, not merely a cookie grant. Children and high-impact data require separate assessment.

Decision: opt-in choice for ordinary consent-based trackers; no automatic SG opt-out variant. A no-prompt example for genuinely nonpersonal analytics or established lawful exceptions is possible without claiming a new national opt-out rule. A timed notification feature would need explicit notice timestamp, opt-out period, purpose/version, pending deny, elapsed-period activation, withdrawal, and assessment evidence outside the engine.

## Malaysia

Applicability correction: Act 709 sections 2 and 3 concern commercial
transactions, establishment or use of equipment in Malaysia, and overseas
processing intended for further processing in Malaysia. Visitor location
alone does not establish applicability. Foreign hosting alone does not rule
it out. Keep both Malaysia helpers as editable processing recipes and override
their matchers according to the actual business and processing scope.

Section 45(2)(c) leaves the Security, Retention and Data Integrity Principles
applicable. Its statistics exemption does not authorize vendor reuse.

The [2026 Data Protection by Design guideline](https://www.pdp.gov.my/ppdpv1/wp-content/uploads/2026/04/Data-Protection-By-Design-Guideline-DpbD.pdf)
recommends opt-in where consent is the basis. Its café website example starts
with strictly necessary cookies only. This newer guidance must be assessed
alongside the older General Code; the Code is not blanket permission for
page-load optional cookies. These passages were read from the official PDF.

Act 709 section 6 requires consent except listed necessary contract/precontract/legal/vital/justice/statutory-function cases; it does not contain a general GDPR-like legitimate-interest ground. Section 7 requires written notice including purposes, recipients, rights and optional/mandatory status in Bahasa Malaysia and English. Section 38 withdrawal; section 43 direct-marketing refusal; section 40 sensitive data explicit consent subject to exceptions. Ordinary consent is not defined as universally explicit.
https://www.pdp.gov.my/ppdpv1/en/akta/pdp-act-2010-en/

Regulations2013 regulation3 requires consent in a form recorded and maintained, a distinguishable consent request when combined with other matters, and proof held by the controller; parental/guardian consent under18. Official regulator landing page links the gazette:
https://www.pdp.gov.my/ppdpv1/en/akta/personal-data-protection-regulations-2013/

JPDP's general notice guide, pp 6-9 English section, explicitly separates notices from consent collection and warns against blanket consent. Cookies/beacons must be disclosed, with clear accessible notice and separate maintained consent evidence.
https://www.pdp.gov.my/ppdpv1/wp-content/uploads/2024/07/Panduan-Penyediaan-Notis-PDP-2022-compressed.pdf

Binding sector code, communications, Part 3 paragraphs 1.3-1.4 permits express or implied consent and, for commencing/continuing contracts, opt-in/opt-out or conduct if recorded and maintained. This is useful proof that Malaysia is not universally affirmative-click consent, but cannot be generalized to every site's unseen ad tags.
https://www.pdp.gov.my/ppdpv1/wp-content/uploads/2024/07/KOD-TATA-AMALAN-PERLINDUNGAN-DATA-PERIBADI-UNTUK-SEKTOR-KOMUNIKASI-ENGLISH-VERSION.pdf

Act 709 section 45(2)(c) explicitly exempts statistics/research-only processing from General, Notice/Choice, Disclosure and Access principles if there is no other processing purpose and published results do not identify subjects. This supports no-banner statistics-only analytics, but not ad profiling or vendor reuse. Primary Act PDF printed English page45, PDF page141:
https://www.pdp.gov.my/ppdpv1/wp-content/uploads/2024/07/UNDANG-UNDANG-MALAYSIA_AKTA_PERLINDUNGAN_DATA_PERIBADI_2010_709_MALAY_AND-ENG_V2022.pdf

Amendment A1727 commenced in phases on 1 January,1 April and1 June 2025, verified official commencement instrument.
https://www.pdp.gov.my/ppdpv1/wp-content/uploads/2024/12/PENETAPAN-TARIKH-PERMULAAN-KUAT-KUASA.pdf

Decision: retain opt-in choice and document contextual opt-out configurations under the applicable code. The General Code recognizes non-objection, voluntary provision and continued service use as possible evidence; the review below gives its scope and notice/recording conditions. This does not establish automatic permission for arbitrary trackers from location alone. Do not ship a blanket Malaysia opt-out notice preset merely because implied consent exists. Necessity or nonpersonal analytics may need no consent banner, while privacy information remains.

## Thailand

PDPA 2019 section 5 covers domestic controllers/processors and foreign offerings or behavior monitoring of people in Thailand. Section 6 personal identifiability definition. Section 19 requires prior or contemporaneous consent unless statutory permission, an explicit written/electronic request, free choice and withdrawal as easy as giving. Section 23 information at/before collection. Section 24 provides alternative bases, including contract, lawful duties, safeguarded statistics and balanced legitimate interests. Section 26 sensitive categories require explicit consent or a specified exception. Section 20 rules for minors differ by capacity and require parental consent below10. Sections28-29 transfers require safeguards/conditions. Section 32 gives objection, including direct marketing. These provisions do not prescribe a cookie-popup shape.
https://www.etda.or.th/getattachment/e820df2c-848f-4e03-86cb-a9dad38cc713/ENG-Version.aspx

Decision: opt-in for consent-based personal analytics/ads; necessary operations may use another basis and truly nonpersonal analytics falls outside personal-data rules. A universal optional-tracking exemption was not found. An opt-out/no-prompt custom config can implement already assessed section 24 processing, not establish the legal assessment. Keep no country-wide immediate-allow ad preset.

## Indonesia

Law 27/2022 primary full text, Articles 2,4,9,20-25,34,40,56,74:
https://jdih.komdigi.go.id/produk_hukum/view/id/832/t/undangundang%2Bnomor%2B27%2Btahun%2B2022
Article 20 expressly allows six bases including balanced legitimate interests; consent must be explicit for disclosed specific purposes. Articles 21-24 require details, recorded/written consent, distinguishability and proof. Article 25 children require parent/guardian consent. Article 40 requires stopping consent-based processing within 3x24 hours after withdrawal. Article 56 overseas transfers prioritizes comparable protection, then adequate binding safeguards, then consent if those conditions unavailable. Article 74 gave two years to adapt from 2022. No broad cookie-storage-only opt-in rule or ordinary analytics exemption identified. Specific data includes health, biometrics, genetics, crime, children and financial information; Article 34 high-risk assessments go beyond category toggles.

Decision: retain opt-in for consent-based trackers. Independent assessed bases can avoid consent banners, but do not infer them from country, category label, or a privacy-notice dismissal. Constitutional Court 137/PUU-XXIII/2025, 19 January 2026, rejected the Article 56 transfer challenge in full according to the primary ministry case record: https://jdih.komdigi.go.id/judicial/view/48 .

## Philippines

DPA2012 section 3,12,13 and IRR 19,21,22 establish personal information scope, multiple bases including legitimate interests, and distinct sensitive-data conditions. Necessary contractual operations do not require cookie consent solely because a cookie is used. Truly anonymized processing outside identifiable personal information is different from pseudonymous cookie IDs.
https://privacy.gov.ph/data-privacy-act/
https://privacy.gov.ph/implementing-rules-regulations-data-privacy-act-2012/

NPC 2023-04, sections 9-12, requires specific informed free express assent and evidence, rejects inferred/nonresponse consent. Section 10(C), however, allows continued use of a specifically described service as assent when all consent requirements are present and prior information explains the act. Do not repeat the older absolute claim that continued use can never count. Section 9 discourages unnecessary consent requests when another basis applies.
https://privacy.gov.ph/wp-content/uploads/2023/11/NPC-Circular-No.-2023-04_Guidelines-on-Consent_07Nov2023.pdf

NPC 2023-07 requires a documented purpose/necessity/balancing assessment, disclosures, ongoing review and scrutiny of the recipient's interests. It cannot authorize sensitive or privileged information. No cookie category automatically passes that test.
https://privacy.gov.ph/wp-content/uploads/2024/01/NPC-Circular-No.-2023-07_Guidelines-on-Legitimate-Interest_13-December-2023.pdf

NPC 2017-047 specifically says cookie information popups are optional when privacy information is adequate, accessible and comprehensible. It concerns notice format, not silent consent for personal advertising.
https://privacy.gov.ph/wp-content/uploads/2022/01/NPC_AdvisoryOpinionNo._2017-047.pdf

Decision: opt-in for consent-based tracking; no banner for reviewed nonconsent processing, with adequate notice and objection. Immediate page-load allow is not the informed continued-use sequence. Avoid a broad country opt-out default. Current consent/LI circulars outweigh older isolated opinions.

## Vietnam

Law 91/2025/QH15 effective 1 January 2026, primary official metadata and signed scanned law:
https://vanban.chinhphu.vn/?classid=1&docid=214590&pageid=27160
https://datafiles.chinhphu.vn/cpp/files/vbpq/2025/7/91qh.signed.pdf
Verified against the signed scan, printed pages 6 and 17. Article 9 requires voluntary informed purpose-specific consent in clear verifiable form; no silence or nonresponse, no forced unrelated purposes. Article 10 withdrawal/restriction requests. Article 28(8) specifically requires consent before behavioral/personalized ad collection through monitoring websites/apps, plus refusal mechanisms and retention/deletion controls. Article 29(3)-(4) requires cookie refusal and no-tracking options for social networking/online communication providers; do not misstate this as universal US-GPC support. Article 19 exceptions need purpose review. Generic Accept All cannot satisfy every separate high-risk processing duty.

Implementing Decree 356/2025 effective 1 January 2026, Article 6 visually read on printed pages 5-6: consent methods must let the controller verify the subject, time and content of assent. Websites/apps can use technical consent controls. Article 6(2) requires retained consent evidence with controller burden; Article 6(3) prohibits default-consent mechanisms or ambiguous/misleading instructions; Article 6(4) requires notice when data is sensitive. This reinforces recorded affirmative permission. Article 5 withdrawal/restriction generally requires a response within 2 working days and implementation within 15 days, or 20 days where processor/third-party involvement is needed, subject to specified extension. These operational response limits do not mean browser tracking should continue after a toggle is withdrawn.
Primary decree:
https://congbao.chinhphu.vn/van-ban/nghi-dinh-so-356-2025-nd-cp-468371.htm
https://datafiles.chinhphu.vn/cpp/files/vbpq/2026/01/356-nd.signed.pdf

Decision: keep opt-in; no immediate-allow optional analytics/targeting preset. An essential service exception is not permission for cross-site profiling.

## Brunei

PDPO 2025 sections 8-17 and Schedule 1 Part 3:
https://pdp.aiti.gov.bn/media/tndpduml/s-1_2025-e.pdf
Section 8 actual/deemed consent or law; section 11 voluntary reasonable provision and contract-linked deemed consent; section 12 notification requires assessment, effective notice, reasonable opt-out period before reliance and excludes direct marketing messages; section 13 withdrawal on reasonable notice; section 17 purpose information. Schedule 1 Part 3 permits assessed legitimate interests outweighing adverse effects, with accessible information and mitigation; it excludes direct marketing messages. Necessary/requested operations can fit actual/deemed consent or exceptions. No general cookie-storage rule found. Section 24 transfer limitation requires comparable protection. These mechanisms are not fulfilled by showing OK while activating all optional tags.

Commencement of substantive duties 1 January 2026, official gazette notification:
https://www.agc.gov.bn/AGC%20Images/LAWS/Gazette_PDF/2025/EN/S%2011_2025%5BE%5D.pdf

Decision: opt-in for generic consent-based trackers; no generic Brunei opt-out. A separately assessed nonconsent use can omit banner; timed notification cannot be represented by present immediate allow.

## Laos

Official Ministry of Industry and Commerce English publication of Law 25/NA, 12 May 2017, promulgated 23 June 2017:
https://lsp.moic.gov.la/?id=289&r=site%2Fdisplaylegal
Articles 2-3 use a broad electronic-data framework including legal entities' data, unlike GDPR. Article 6 domestic/international actors located or active in Laos. Articles 9-10 distinguish general/specific data; Article 12 requires owner approval and purpose/detail notice to owner and administration authorities before collection. Article 16 use/disclosure permission with legal exceptions. Article 17 transfer permission and recipient security, including overseas. Articles 19-20 stop transfers/update/delete requests. Article 33 restricts sensitive subject areas. No express ordinary-analytics, necessary-cookie or anonymous-statistics exemption verified in the available text; do not transplant European exceptions. Translation has omissions/awkwardness, and implementing 2018 instruction remains unverified.

Decision: opt-in approval starter for optional tracking, with transfer/other obligations separately handled. Do not certify a no-banner/opt-out national alternative from these sources. This review has lower precision for Laos than Singapore/Philippines; mark local-law review needed.

## Additional source checks

- Brunei commencement instrument S11/2025 was verified against the Attorney General's gazette. It commences Parts 3-9, section 42 and Schedules 1-5 on 1 January 2026. This includes consent duties. The statute does not establish a general GDPR-style sensitive-category/child consent-age model; no precise child age threshold verified here. Do not invent one in metadata.
- Singapore business-improvement exception is for use of personal data already lawfully collected, with necessity of identifiable form and reasonable appropriateness. Group sharing has additional conditions. It is not free permission to collect new site analytics from every anonymous visitor. Source Key Concepts 2026 pp 69-73.
- Malaysia's General Code was checked against the official scan. It supports contextual implied-consent and opt-out routes subject to the conditions below.

## Cross-check of Malaysia and Singapore

Malaysia correction: the General Code, not only the communications-sector code, recognizes consent through non-objection, voluntary provision and continued service use (§3.3.3(b)). It also permits certain notified direct marketing and an opt-out route (§10.6.3–4). Therefore "Malaysia always requires an affirmative button" and "implied consent exists only for telecom" are wrong. The code applies to relevant classes lacking their own code (§1.1.2). It still requires proper recorded consent and rejects a privacy notice as blanket consent (§4.2). These provisions were visually verified in the regulator's scanned PDF, printed pages 5, 11–12, 36–37.

Recommendation: document a contextual Malaysian opt-out recipe, with host review of code applicability, notice, evidence of conduct, purpose, recipients, and sensitive/child data. Do not ship an unrestricted `malaysiaOptOut` whose immediate category permissions follow from country alone. This is a product-scope conclusion, not a claim that all initial tracking needs an affirmative click. The law provides meaningful implied-consent routes; geolocation and an unrecorded notice do not establish their facts for arbitrary analytics and advertising SDKs. [Official General Code](https://www.pdp.gov.my/ppdpv1/en/akta/personal-data-protection-general-practice/).

Singapore correction: avoid calling the whole jurisdiction opt-in. The legitimate-interests exception can authorize collection, use and disclosure after an assessment and disclosure of reliance. Business improvement can permit analytics on lawfully collected existing-customer data; it does not generally authorize fresh third-party collection. The regulator distinguishes preparatory analytics from sending direct marketing messages, for which these exceptions are unavailable. Deemed consent by notification also requires a reasonable opt-out period before processing, so immediate default-allowed categories do not implement that workflow. A documented assessed-analytics/no-prompt recipe is defensible; country-wide default permission for arbitrary personal tracking/third-party advertising is not established. The targeted-cookie consent guidance remains directly relevant, and cannot be replaced by the general existence of exceptions. [PDPC Key Concepts, revised 29 April 2026, legitimate interests and business improvement sections including pp 62–74](https://www.pdpc.gov.sg/-/media/files/pdpc/pdf-files/advisory-guidelines/ag-on-key-concepts/advisory-guidelines-on-key-concepts-in-the-pdpa-17-may-2022.pdf); [PDPC Selected Topics, cookie guidance §7.5–7.14](https://www.pdpc.gov.sg/-/media/files/pdpc/pdf-files/advisory-guidelines/ag-on-selected-topics/advisory-guidelines-on-the-pdpa-for-selected-topics-(revised-may-2024).pdf).
